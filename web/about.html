About

Property numbers you can check

MarketStats is a free analytics site for the NSW property market. It turns official public datasets into clear rent, price, yield, gearing and trend analysis for every postcode.

What MarketStats is

Most property heatmaps show one number per suburb and stop there. MarketStats shows median weekly rent by dwelling type and bedrooms, median sale prices, gross rental yield, a typical investor's cash flow and gearing at today's average investor interest rate, and nine years of quarterly trends for every NSW postcode with enough data. You can open any postcode, compare it over time, and test a purchase with your own loan, costs and tax rate.

MarketStats currently covers New South Wales. Other states will follow where equivalent open data exists.

Who it's for

Investors

Compare yields and estimated cash flow across postcodes, see where rents are rising fastest, and check whether a typical purchase would be positively or negatively geared at the current rate.

Buyers

See median prices for strata and non-strata homes, how many sales there were, and how prices have moved since 2017.

Renters

Find out what a 1, 2, 3 or 4+ bedroom home actually rents for in a postcode this quarter, and how fast rents are changing.

Built on official public data

Every number on MarketStats comes from an official public dataset that is published under an open licence allowing commercial reuse, or is calculated from one using a method we publish:

  • NSW Department of Communities and Justice: Rent and Sales Report (rental bonds and property sales by postcode).
  • Australian Bureau of Statistics: postcode, suburb and SA2 boundaries, and 2021 Census data.
  • Reserve Bank of Australia: average interest rate on new investor home loans (Table F6).
  • Australian Prudential Regulation Authority: share of new loans by loan-to-value ratio, used to pick a typical deposit.
  • Australian Institute of Health and Welfare: NSW housing assistance indicators such as rental stress among Rent Assistance recipients and the social housing waitlist.

We never fill gaps with estimates or scraped listings. Where a source suppresses or flags small samples, we hide or fade those areas. See Data & method for every source, its licence and how each figure is calculated.

How often it updates

The DCJ Rent and Sales Report is published quarterly, about one to two quarters after the period it covers. MarketStats rebuilds when a new report comes out, and refreshes the RBA interest rate each month. AIHW housing assistance figures are annual, at 30 June. Current data: rents for the latest quarter, sales for the latest quarter, interest rate as at the latest month. Last built recently.
