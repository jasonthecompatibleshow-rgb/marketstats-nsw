Data limitations and sample sizes

  • Rents come from new rental bonds lodged with NSW Fair Trading. They describe new leases in the quarter, not all existing rents, and exclude tenancies where no bond was lodged.
  • Sale prices are the property sales compiled and published by DCJ in the Rent and Sales Report, by contract date. Recent quarters are incomplete and are usually revised as late settlements are recorded.
  • DCJ suppresses results for 10 or fewer bonds or sales and flags 11–30 as small samples. MarketStats hides suppressed areas, fades small samples on the map, and leaves them out of rankings by default. A small sample can still swing sharply from quarter to quarter.
  • A few isolated historical points that are more than 2.5 times (or less than 0.4 times) their neighbouring quarters are treated as data errors and left out of trend charts and historical comparisons. Latest-quarter figures always use the published values.
  • Postcodes are ABS postal areas, which approximate Australia Post delivery areas. Suburb names listed for a postcode are the 2021 Census suburbs that overlap it, ordered by population.
  • Census figures are from 2021 and may be out of date.
  • AIHW housing assistance figures are NSW state-level (or SA2-level where labelled), not postcode figures. Rent Assistance counts are rounded by AIHW and only cover people receiving Commonwealth Rent Assistance.
  • Source data can contain errors and can be revised. We republish it as supplied, apart from the steps described on the Data & method page.

Medians and yields are approximations

  • A median is the middle value of what was rented or sold in a postcode in a quarter. It is not the value of any particular property, and it changes with the mix of properties that happened to trade.
  • Gross yield is median weekly rent × 52 ÷ median sale price. DCJ does not split sales by bedrooms, so yields compare a bedroom-specific rent with an all-bedroom price: units and townhouses use strata sales, houses use non-strata sales. Use yields to compare areas, not to value a property.
  • Yields ignore vacancy, costs and purchase costs. Net yield and cash flow in the calculator use the assumptions below.

Gearing and cash flow are modelled

The "typical investor" figures are a model, not a measurement. They assume:

  • purchase at the postcode's median price for that dwelling type, with a 20% deposit (80% LVR) and no purchase costs or stamp duty;
  • the RBA average variable rate on new investor loans (current, latest month), principal and interest over 30 years, using first-year interest;
  • median new-bond rent with 2 weeks' vacancy a year, 7% management, 5% maintenance, and fixed allowances for strata, council, water and insurance by dwelling type;
  • no land tax by default (land value under the general threshold) and no depreciation;
  • "positively" or "negatively" geared is based on the rental tax result (rent received minus costs and interest), with ±$20 a week treated as neutral. Cash flow also subtracts principal repayments.

Your loan, rate, costs, property and tax position will differ. After-tax figures are rough estimates using a flat marginal rate plus the Medicare levy, and ignore offsets, other income, capital gains and changes in tax law.
