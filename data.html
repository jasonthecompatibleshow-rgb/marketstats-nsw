AIHW housing indicators

The AIHW publishes most housing assistance data for states and territories, not postcodes. MarketStats shows these as NSW state-level context and always labels the geography. They are never shown as if they were postcode figures.

  • Rental stress (CRA.9): share of income units receiving Commonwealth Rent Assistance who still pay more than 30% of gross income on rent, at the last Friday in June.
  • Rent Assistance by SA2 (CRA.2b): number of recipient income units in each ABS SA2 area at June 2023, 2024 and 2025. On the map, a postcode's panel lists the SA2 areas that overlap it (with the share of the postcode's area each covers). These are SA2 figures, not postcode figures: SA2 and postcode boundaries don't line up, so we don't split or add them.
  • Social housing share (SOCIALSHARE.1): social housing households ÷ all households, calculated by MarketStats from the two counts AIHW publishes.
  • Public housing waitlist (HOUSEHOLDS.28) and social housing dwellings (DWELLINGS.2), both from the AIHW National Housing Assistance Data Repository.
  • AIHW rounds Rent Assistance counts to the nearest 5. Figures update once a year, usually mid-year.

What we don't use, and why

  • Cotality (formerly CoreLogic) home value and rent indices shown on the Australian Government's Housing Data Dashboard. These are third-party data licensed to government and excluded from the dashboard's Creative Commons licence. Cotality's terms don't allow commercial reuse or redistribution.
  • AIHW table HOUSEHOLDS.26 (consolidated social housing waitlists). Its stated source is "state and territory supplied data" rather than an AIHW collection. To stay on the safe side of AIHW's third-party exclusion, we leave it out and use the AIHW-collected public housing waitlist (HOUSEHOLDS.28) instead.
  • AIHW logos, the Commonwealth Coat of Arms and any material marked as third-party, which AIHW's licence excludes.
  • Listings, asking rents and automated valuations from property portals. Their terms don't allow it, so MarketStats doesn't scrape them.

Rents and prices

Rents are DCJ's median weekly rent for new bonds lodged in the quarter, by postcode, dwelling type (all, house, unit/flat, townhouse) and bedrooms. Prices are DCJ's median sale price by postcode for all, strata and non-strata dwellings, by contract date. We read the published medians directly and do not re-calculate them. Results DCJ suppresses (10 or fewer) are shown as no data. Results DCJ flags as 11–30 are faded and marked *.

Trends and top movers

We parse every quarterly DCJ issue from Sep 2017 to the latest (– quarters). Earlier issues used a different method and aren't comparable. Each series is lightly cleaned: a point more than 2.5× or less than 0.4× the median of the four quarters either side is treated as an error and dropped. 12-month and 5-year changes compare the same quarter in each year. Insights rankings only include postcodes that pass the chosen minimum sample in every quarter compared.

Gross yield

Gross yield = median weekly rent × 52 ÷ median sale price. DCJ doesn't split sales by bedrooms, so units and townhouses are compared with strata sales, houses with non-strata sales, and "all" with all sales. Historical yields use the rent and price from the same quarter.

Typical-investor gearing and cash flow

For each postcode and dwelling type we model a typical purchase at the median price with a 80% LVR loan at the RBA rate (–), P&I over 30 years. We subtract 2 weeks' vacancy, 7% management, 5% maintenance and allowances for strata, council, water and insurance.

  • Rental tax result = rent received − running costs − first-year interest. Above +$20/wk is positively geared; below −$20/wk is negatively geared; in between is neutral.
  • Pre-tax cash flow = rent received − running costs − total repayments (interest and principal).
  • Break-even rate = the interest rate at which the rental tax result would be exactly zero, holding everything else fixed.
  • The gearing map colours postcodes by how far the weekly tax result is above or below zero, so you can see how deeply negative each area is.

Every assumption can be changed in the calculator on the map. See the disclaimer for limits.

Geography

Postcodes are ABS 2021 Postal Areas, matched to DCJ postcodes. Suburb names are 2021 Suburbs and Localities that overlap each postcode by at least a small share of their area, ordered by Census population. SA2 overlaps for AIHW data use the same boundary files, projected to equal-area (EPSG:3577), and list SA2s covering at least 5% of the postcode.
